The Exponential Time Hypothesis, and OpenAI's claim that it is false

For Hacker News readers (novice in complexity theory) · about 12 minutes of the reader's time · scrollytelling in 9 chapters, ToC rail on desktop

Shape: A story told in order: a puzzle → the wall it hits → the bet that the wall is permanent (ETH) → what was built on the bet → the claim that breaks it → the fallout → how far to trust it. Sticky canvases advance as you scroll (presentational, as requested). Predict-then-reveal cards sit at the key ideas and at each misconception, so the reader doesn't just watch. Rejected: a full-page explorable or SAT-solver sandbox. It's slower to learn, the user asked for presentation, and the new algorithm's speedup can't be shown at any size a browser can run.

Framing (d-02): it is always "OpenAI's claim", never "the proof that ETH is false". Press-only details (dates, "practical gains need further work") are attributed as reported. No count of dependent papers is given.

Objectives:

  • o-1 Explain 3-SAT with a concrete example, and why brute force takes ~2^n. checks: k-01, k-02
  • o-2 State ETH in plain words and tell it apart from P≠NP. checks: k-03, k-08
  • o-3 Explain why ETH matters (conditional lower bounds) with an example or two. checks: k-04, k-06, k-08
  • o-4 Explain what OpenAI claims, its verification status, and what would and wouldn't follow. checks: k-05, k-06, k-07, k-08

check_plan warnings: none

s-02

The puzzle: three switches, four rules

concrete → predicto-1

Solve a 3-SAT instance by hand before it is named.

"Here are three switches and four rules. Every rule must be on. Try it."

After the sweep: "That's 3-SAT: rules that are ORs of at most three switches, all ANDed together. The question is whether any setting satisfies all of them. Checking every setting is brute force: 2^n rows for n switches."

Collapsed: "How to read NOT and OR".

PREDICT · k-01
There are 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 ways to set the three switches. How many of them light all four rules? Options: None of them / Exactly 2 / About half (4) / All 8.
Feedback: Exactly 2: (F,F,T) and (F,T,T). The only way to be sure was to check every setting, which is brute force. Every extra switch doubles the table.

s-03

The doubling wall

scrolly worked example · B1o-1

Make 2^n visceral. Show the best known algorithm only shrinks the base.

Step 1, n=3: "8 rows. You just did it."

Step 2, n=20: "About a million. A laptop doesn't notice."

Step 3, n=100: "About 10^30. A billion computers, each checking a billion rows a second: tens of thousands of years."

[k-02 gate] Step 4, n=110: "×1,024."

Step 5, n=1000: "10^301, more than the atoms in the observable universe (~10^80)." Then: "Clever algorithms do better: the best known is about 1.307^n. That's a smaller base, but still a wall."

PREDICT · k-02
Brute force on 100 switches already needs about 10^30 checks. Add 10 more switches. How much more work is that? Options: About 10% more (tempting) / About 10× more / About 1,000× more.
Feedback: 2^10 = 1,024. 110 is only 10% bigger than 100, but each switch doubles the table, ten times over.

s-04

The bet: "you can't get under the wall"

ladder · B1 → applyo-2

State ETH as an unproven assumption. Show on a ladder that it forbids more than P≠NP does.

"In 1999 Russell Impagliazzo and Ramamohan Paturi made a bet: no algorithm will ever get 3-SAT under the wall. Some exponential, c^n with c above 1, is unavoidable. That's the Exponential Time Hypothesis. Nobody proved it. It's an assumption, and a lot got built on it."

Ladder steps: (1) the rungs; (2) the P≠NP zone ("not polynomial"); (3) the ETH zone ("not even subexponential"); (4) "ETH is the stronger bet: it forbids more."

Collapsed: "The formal statement" (some s>0 such that every algorithm needs 2^(s·n); no 2^{o(n)} algorithm).

APPLY · k-03
Suppose someone proved that 3-SAT can be solved in about 2^√n steps. Which assumption would be broken? Options: P ≠ NP (so P = NP) (tempting) / ETH only / Both / Neither.
Feedback: 2^√n is below every c^n, so it's in ETH's forbidden zone, but above every polynomial. A broken ETH alone says nothing about P vs NP.

Shaded bands with names printed inside: "P≠NP says: not down here" (polynomial only) and a wider "ETH says: not anywhere down here" (everything below c^n). A gap is left where s-06's rung will go. s-05

What was built on the bet

worked conditional → explaino-3

Show that ETH is load-bearing, and that SETH is a stronger cousin underneath diff.

"Complexity theorists can rarely prove a problem is hard. What they can prove is: if this were much faster, 3-SAT would be too. So: 'Graph colouring has no 2^{o(n)} algorithm, unless ETH fails.'"

The same move gives Hamiltonian cycle, vertex cover, and k-Clique (n^{o(k)}: brute-force search is essentially optimal). Many results take this form; no count is given.

"There's an even stronger bet, SETH: as rules get wider, nothing beats brute force by much. It underwrites a famous one: edit distance, the engine of diff, can't be done much faster than quadratic."

Collapsed: "Why is SETH stronger?"

EXPLAIN · k-04 · think, then reveal
A paper proves "graph colouring has no 2^{o(n)} algorithm, unless ETH fails." In your own words: what did it actually prove, and what would make the statement stop telling us anything about hardness?
Model answer: It proved a link: a much faster colouring algorithm would give a much faster 3-SAT algorithm. Colouring is "hard" only while ETH holds. If ETH fails, the statement is still true but no longer shows hardness.

s-06

The claim

statement → ladder → predict → charto-4

Say exactly what's claimed and where it lands: just under the wall, nowhere near polynomial.

"OpenAI's claim: a deterministic algorithm that counts the solutions of any 3-SAT formula, or any fixed-width k-SAT, in roughly

2^( n / (log n)^{1/2 + o(1)} )

steps, times a polynomial, using only polynomial memory. If you can count solutions you can tell whether there is one, so the 3-SAT case is a 2^{o(n)} algorithm. That's ETH's forbidden zone."

The timeline strip comes next. Collapsed: "What the paper actually proves" (fixed k; deterministic; polynomial space; also refutes SETH, Gap-ETH and NSETH).

PREDICT · k-05
The claimed algorithm is subexponential, and the old best was exponential. When do you expect it to beat the old best (1.307^n) on real formulas? Options: Immediately: SAT solvers get much faster (tempting) / Somewhere around a few hundred variables / Only at enormous sizes, if ever in practice, and nobody knows the real constants.
Feedback: With every constant generously set to 1, the bound only edges past 1.307^n around n ≈ 1,000 (sparse formulas), or n ≈ 10^83 (general). The exponent shrinks by only ~√log n. Real solvers exploit structure, not worst-case bounds. Press reports OpenAI saying practical gains need substantial further work.

s-07

If it holds: what falls, what stands

predict → misconceptions · B4o-3o-4

Work through the consequences: unproven (not false) theorems, SETH falls too, OVC survivors, no P=NP, no crypto break.

"Back to the foundation. If ETH goes, what happens to everything stacked on it?"

After k-06, the diagram cracks. "These theorems aren't wrong. They're conditional statements whose condition has failed. They're unproven again. Nobody has found a fast algorithm for colouring or diff."

Three myth cards, shown together (not a carousel).

PREDICT · k-06
The famous result that edit distance (the core of diff) can't be done much faster than quadratic time assumes SETH, not ETH. If OpenAI's claim holds, what happens to that result? Options: Untouched: only ETH was refuted (tempting) / It loses its basis: SETH falls too / Edit distance now has a fast algorithm.
Feedback: SETH implies ETH, so ETH false means SETH false, and the paper says so. But "loses its basis" isn't "now fast". Some cousins (longest common subsequence, regex matching) can still lean on OVC, which this doesn't touch.

s-08

Is it true? What "verified" does and doesn't mean

contrast · B4 → explaino-4

Teach the reader to read "verified in Lean" correctly, and what to watch for.

"'Verified in Lean' is strong evidence, and it's also narrower than it sounds."

After the table: the review group's own words, as reported: "Our involvement should not be taken as certification of the claim." Base rate: the same repo withdrew three unrelated manuscripts on 7 Oct after a sign error.

What to watch for: the working group's write-up · whether the Lean definitions are judged faithful · independent re-checks · whether the paper's sharper bound holds.

Collapsed: "The exact formal bound" (Lean proves 2^{A·n(log log n)²/√log n}, slightly weaker than the headline but still enough to refute ETH).

EXPLAIN · k-07 · think, then reveal
Lean's checker accepted the proof. Name one way the result could still turn out to be wrong, or weaker than advertised.
Model answer: Lean confirms that the formal theorem follows. Humans must confirm that the formal definitions (machine model, formula encoding, what "time" counts) mean what the paper says. A subtly off definition could make the checked theorem say less than the headline. That's why ETH's own inventors are reviewing it, and why they say their involvement isn't a certification.

s-09

The whole story in five statements

interleaved retrieval → recapo-2o-3o-4

Retrieve across all objectives, then close.

SORT · k-08 · each statement committed separately
Suppose the claim survives review. For each statement, does it follow?
(a) P = NP → doesn't follow (far above polynomial)
(b) 3-SAT can be solved in 2^{o(n)} time → follows (that's the claim)
(c) RSA encryption is broken → doesn't follow (never rested on ETH; subexponential ≠ fast)
(d) Theorems proved "unless ETH fails" become unproven, not false → follows
(e) SETH is false too → follows (SETH implies ETH)
Each answer's feedback points back to its chapter.

Recap (4 lines): 3-SAT is the switches puzzle, and brute force doubles per switch. ETH bet that no algorithm gets under an exponential wall. Many hardness results were built on that bet. OpenAI claims an algorithm just under the wall: if review confirms it, those results become unproven again, SETH falls too, and P vs NP and your crypto are untouched.

Further reading: OpenAI's math repo (manuscript, Lean files) · Wikipedia, "Exponential time hypothesis".
